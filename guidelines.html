Traveler's Code of Conduct

Himachal Travel Guidelines & Mountain Wisdom

The Himalayas offer boundless peace and breathtaking panoramas, but require preparedness. Follow these vital guidelines on high-altitude acclimatization, mountain road safety, eco-rules, and interactive gear checklist.

Crucial Protocols

The Five Golden Rules of Mountain Travel

1. Acclimatization & AMS Protocol

When traveling to high altitudes above 2,500m (Spiti Valley, Rohtang, Atal Tunnel, Jalori Pass, Dainkund Peak), allow your body 24-48 hours to acclimatize.

  • • Drink 3 to 4 liters of warm water daily. Stay well hydrated.
  • • Never ignore AMS symptoms: headache, nausea, loss of appetite, dizziness.
  • • If symptoms worsen, descend to a lower altitude immediately.

2. Mountain Driving & Curve Etiquette

Roads in Himachal (from Pathankot-Dalhousie to Mandi-Manali) wind along steep gorges and hairpins.

  • • Uphill Priority: The ascending vehicle always has the right of way. Give way gracefully.
  • • Engine Braking: Never coast downhill in neutral. Downshift to 2nd or 1st gear to prevent brake pad overheating.
  • • Lightly tap your horn on blind corners to alert oncoming traffic.

3. Zero Plastic & Eco Compliance

Himachal Pradesh is a green state that rigorously enforces bans on non-biodegradable polythene and single-use plastic.

  • • Carry reusable stainless steel flasks. Tap water in mountain towns is natural spring water and readily filterable.
  • • Littering in meadows like Khajjiar or trails incurs heavy fines. Bring all your trash back to your vehicle.
  • • Never pluck alpine flowers or deface ancient pine trees.

4. Temple & Village Deota Respect

Himachal is revered as 'Devbhoomi' (Land of Gods). Each hamlet has its guardian deity (Gram Deota).

  • • Remove footwear, leather belts, and hats before stepping into temple complexes.
  • • Always walk clockwise (Kora) around Buddhist shrines, stupas, and Khajji Nag pagoda.
  • • In remote Kullu and Chamba hamlets, avoid touching sacred wooden structures without local permission.

5. Permits & Checkpoint Rules

Certain high-altitude passes and border-adjacent valleys require official clearance before departure.

  • • Rohtang Pass: Mandatory online vehicle permit through the Kullu district administration portal.
  • • Inner Line Permit (ILP): Non-Indian passport holders require ILP for travel between Khab, Nako, and Spiti.
  • • Drone flying without prior DC permission is banned near military establishments and dams.

6. Monsoon & Winter Driving

Himalayan weather changes rapidly. Check official weather bulletins before heading up mountain roads.

  • • Monsoon (Jul-Aug): Avoid active landslide belts. Rivers swell rapidly; stay off boulder beds.
  • • Winter (Dec-Feb): Beware of black ice on shaded roads in early mornings. Carry snow chains in Khajjiar/Kufri.
  • • Keep a 1-day buffer in your itinerary for sudden weather halts.
